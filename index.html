Locked rulesApplied to every image automatically
  • Locked style. Professional photography, clean natural light, native 4K.
  • Look. Refined, old-money minimal wardrobe for every age (tailored trousers, crisp shirts, fine knits, clean blazers, loafers), never streetwear. Colours only from the BAE palette, never teal, never orange. Bold & colourful: crisp white or black layers over one fresh colour piece (blush, raspberry, burgundy or sky blue). Elegant & calm: warm, tonal ink, slate, burgundy, dusty rose, stone and ecru.
  • Story. Everyone the action needs is in the frame: a cashier or sales associate when paying, with neatly displayed products. Studio builds a minimal staged version of the place.
  • Wardrobe. Modern and contemporary. No hijab, no headscarf, no traditional Arab clothing. No orange clothing on anyone.
  • Modern Jordan. Today's Amman only: Abdali, new towers, sleek cafés, modern homes and offices. Nothing old or traditional.
  • Skin. Professional retouch: even skin tone, soft glow, subtle real texture, never plastic.
  • Phones. Always a modern iPhone 18, logo hidden.
  • Decor. Modern Scandinavian, IKEA style: light oak, white walls, clean lines, no clutter.
  • Composition lock. The person or group is always centred.
  • Studio lock. Studio puts the shot on a BAE studio set, whatever place the brief names.
  • References. Style only: camera height and angle, lens, depth, light, colour grade and the kind of place. Nothing literal is copied: no place, object, person, pose, outfit or layout.
  • Never. Logos, readable text, watermarks, staged stock poses, heavy filters.
  • 16:9 on Light Stone. The page flood-fills the edges to exact #ECE8DF, so the background is one even colour with no floor or shadow.
  • Glassmorphism + BAE gradient. A frosted, see-through glass element in front of a solid orange-to-burgundy form.
  • Card stacks. Three cards: the top one glass, the middle and bottom ones BAE gradient.
  • Form. Isometric three-quarter view, soft rounded edges, gentle inner glow, one focal point.
  • Never. Text, numbers, logos or the BAE symbol.
